Make a time-lapse of your Cities: Skylines city from old saves

If you export your city now and then, you already have its history. Skyline Map lines those exports up so you can watch the city grow, one save at a time.

November 9, 2026

Keep your exports

A time-lapse needs several exports of the same city. By default each CSLMapView export can overwrite the last one, so first make sure they are kept:

  • Turn on timestamps in file names in the mod's settings. Each export then gets its own file, like Sarrant-20261107-234707.cslmap.
  • Optionally turn on export on save, so a new file appears every time you save the game.

They all land in the same folder (here's where). The more saves you have, and the more spread out they are, the smoother the time-lapse.

Make the time-lapse

  1. Open your newest export at skyline.codz.me.
  2. Open the menu and choose City time-lapse, then Choose older exports.
  3. In the file picker, select your older exports. Selecting the whole folder is fine: files from other cities, and exports newer than the open one, are skipped.
  4. Drag the slider, or press Play.

Reading is quick, because only the identities of roads, buildings and lines are read from the older files, not their shapes. Twenty-eight exports of a large city take a few seconds.

To select many files at once: on a Mac, click the first file and Shift-click the last, or press ⌘A. On Windows, use Ctrl instead of ⌘.

How it works, and its limits

The map you see is always your newest export. Each road, building and transit line in it is matched against the older files, and appears from the first export that already had it.

Cities: Skylines reuses ids after something is demolished, so an id alone could credit a brand-new road to an old one that used the same slot. Skyline Map therefore matches a road by its id and the two junctions it connects, a building by its id and its asset, and a line by its id and type.

Because only the newest export is drawn, anything you demolished along the way doesn't appear in earlier frames. If you rebuilt a whole district, the time-lapse shows the new version appearing, not the old one disappearing.

Ideas

  • Record it. Zoom to a part of town that changed a lot, press Play and screen-record it. Short clips like that are popular on city-builder forums.
  • Track your growth. The building count and road length per export are a quick record of how fast the city grew.
  • Combine with transit. Leave the transit layer on and watch your network spread across the map.
