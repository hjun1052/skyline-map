Blog / City records and oddities

Find the records in your Cities: Skylines city

Every city has its extremes: a bridge longer than the rest, a station deeper than the others, one house miles from anything. Skyline Map finds them in your export and shows each one on the map.

November 9, 2026

Open City discoveries

  1. Open your export at skyline.codz.me.
  2. Press the gold sparkle button in the map controls, or choose City discoveries in the menu.
  3. Pick a record. The map moves to it, draws it in gold and shows its number on a badge.

The arrows step through every record, Play tour moves on by itself every few seconds, and the shuffle button jumps to a random one. On a phone the panel shrinks to the record's card, so the map stays in view.

What it looks for

Transit

  • The longest line of each kind: railway, metro, tram, monorail, bus, ferry and cable car.
  • The busiest interchange, where the most lines stop.
  • The longest run between two stations, and the two stations closest together.
  • The deepest and the highest station, and the line with the biggest climb.
  • The stretch of road or track the most lines share.

Roads and bridges

  • The longest bridge (and how much of it is over water), viaduct, road tunnel and rail tunnel.
  • The longest named road, and the longest stretch that is dead straight.
  • The steepest street, the highest road, and any road below sea level.
  • The junction where the most roads meet, the widest road and the longest runway.

Land, water and buildings

  • The highest point on the map, the deepest water and how much of the map is water.
  • The building with the biggest footprint and the one made of the most parts.
  • The asset you built most often, the building highest above the sea and the loneliest one.

Oddities

  • Level crossings, where a road meets a railway with no bridge or tunnel.
  • Road islands: stretches of road with no road link to the rest of the city.
  • Ghost stations: named and built, but no line stops there.
  • The longest name you gave anything.

Measured, not guessed

Every number comes from your export: lengths from the road and track geometry, heights from the nodes and terrain, counts from the network. The export has no traffic, passenger numbers or building heights, so there are no records about those.

A record only appears when your city has a clear one. A small town gets fewer records than a big city, and a city with no railway has no railway records.
