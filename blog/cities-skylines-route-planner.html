Blog / Directions in your city

Driving, transit, walking and cycling directions in your Cities: Skylines city

Once your city is a map, the next question is how to get around it. Skyline Map plans routes on your real road network and transit lines, the way Google Maps does for a real city.

November 9, 2026

Getting directions

  1. Open your export at skyline.codz.me.
  2. Click Directions, or right-click the map and choose Directions from here or Directions to here.
  3. Pick a start and an end. You can type a station, road or landmark, or click the map.
  4. Choose driving, transit, walking or cycling.

You get alternatives where there are any, turn-by-turn steps, and pins you can drag to change the trip.

Driving

Driving routes use the roads a car may drive on, follow their speed limits and add time at junctions: a little for going straight across, more for a left turn than a right, and most for a U-turn.

One-way streets

The export doesn't record one-way directions reliably. It marks one-way lanes, but leaves out a flag the game uses to flip a road segment's direction, so the raw data alone gets the direction right only 51–89% of the time, depending on the city. Skyline Map works the directions out from more evidence:

  • Confirmed: your buses and other vehicles drive that road that way, or junction rules lock it to such a road. These are never driven against. If no route respects them, Directions says there is no legal driving route instead of drawing a wrong one.
  • Likely: divided roads, where traffic keeps right. These are followed as a rule too. In the rare case where nothing else works, the route is marked with a warning at the spot where it breaks the rule.
  • Uncertain: only the raw value. A route can use these, but it pays a penalty.

Checked against the routes your own transit lines take, the recovered directions come out 97–100% right on the cities we tested.

Where a trip starts

A trip starts and ends partway along the nearest road a car may use, not at the nearest junction, so the route goes round the block when it needs to. Trips don't begin in the middle of a motorway unless there is no other road nearby.

Public transit

Transit routes use your lines and stations by name. Ride times come from the speed limits of the track or road each line runs on, capped at realistic speeds for each kind of vehicle, with time for accelerating, braking and stopping at each station. Buses also lose time at junctions and in traffic.

The export has no timetables, so waiting times are estimates and are labelled that way. If you set fares (see tolls and fares), each option shows its price.

Walking and cycling

Walking routes follow pavements and footpaths, and stay off motorways, platforms and other places pedestrians can't go. They join the path network at the nearest point on a path, so the line doesn't cut straight across a block. If a place can only be reached by motorway, the route says so rather than drawing a path that doesn't exist.

Cycling prefers bike lanes and ordinary roads, and pushes the bike along footpaths when it has to. Turn on the Cycling overlay to see the bike network.

What the export doesn't include

Some things the game knows aren't in a CSLMapView export, so no route can account for them:

  • Traffic lights and turn bans, including Traffic Manager: President Edition (TM:PE) rules.
  • Live traffic.
  • Timetables, which is why transit waits are estimates.

For a closer look at what is and isn't in the file, see what a CSLMapView export contains.
